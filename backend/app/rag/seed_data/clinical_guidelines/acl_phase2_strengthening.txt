ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation: Phase 2 - Controlled Strengthening (Weeks 3-8)

Once full extension and at least 120 degrees of flexion are achieved, and swelling is well controlled, patients progress into closed-chain strengthening: mini squats, leg press, and stationary cycling. Closed-chain exercise is preferred over open-chain resisted extension throughout this phase because it distributes load more evenly across the joint and reduces shear stress on the graft.

Gait should be assessed at every visit; a persistent quadriceps-avoidance gait pattern (reduced knee flexion during stance) at week 6 or later is a flag worth noting, as it is associated with slower strength recovery and should prompt closer gait retraining focus.

By week 8, most patients should demonstrate quadriceps strength at approximately 70% of the contralateral limb on manual muscle testing or simple functional comparison (e.g., single-leg sit-to-stand repetitions), though formal isokinetic testing is not required for MVP-scale protocols.

Single-leg balance work (eyes open, stable surface) can begin as soon as weight-bearing is pain-free, progressing to unstable surfaces later in the phase as tolerated.
