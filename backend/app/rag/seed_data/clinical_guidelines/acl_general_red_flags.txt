ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation: General Red Flags Across All Phases

The following findings, at any point in the rehabilitation timeline, warrant escalation to the surgical team rather than routine continuation of the therapy plan: fever or spreading redness/warmth at the incision site (possible infection), a sudden increase in swelling accompanied by calf pain or tenderness (possible deep vein thrombosis), a mechanical locking or catching sensation in the knee that was not present previously (possible new meniscal or cartilage injury), and any acute pop or giving-way event followed by significant new swelling (possible graft re-injury).

Persistent numbness or tingling along the incision is common and typically not a red flag on its own, since minor saphenous nerve branch irritation is a known, usually self-resolving occurrence after ACL reconstruction. However, new or worsening numbness beyond the immediate post-operative window should still be documented and monitored.

A patient reporting these symptoms should always be redirected to contact the clinic directly rather than receiving reassurance or clinical guidance through any automated interface.
