ACL Reconstruction Rehabilitation: Phase 1 - Protection (Weeks 0-2)

The primary goals of Phase 1 are pain and swelling control, protection of the graft site, and restoration of full passive knee extension. Weight-bearing status is typically determined by the surgical team based on concomitant procedures (e.g., meniscus repair); in the absence of other restrictions, patients generally progress to weight-bearing as tolerated with crutches by the end of week 1.

Range of motion goals: 0 degrees extension and at least 90 degrees flexion by the end of week 2. Loss of full extension in this window is a common and correctable complication if addressed early with prone hangs and heel props; if extension deficit persists past week 3, escalate to the surgical team.

Quadriceps activation (quad sets, straight leg raises without extension lag) should begin as soon as tolerated. A persistent extensor lag on straight leg raise beyond week 2 warrants closer monitoring rather than immediate concern, since early post-operative inhibition is common.

Avoid open-chain resisted knee extension in this phase, as it places excessive anterior shear stress on a healing graft.
