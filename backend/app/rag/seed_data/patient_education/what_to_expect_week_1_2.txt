What to Expect: The First Two Weeks After ACL Reconstruction

It's normal to feel more tired than usual in the first two weeks — your body is working hard to heal. Swelling and some bruising around the knee, and sometimes down into the calf or ankle, is common and usually improves steadily over this period. Keeping your leg elevated above heart level when resting, and using ice regularly, are the two most helpful things you can do at home.

You'll likely be using crutches during this time. Your therapist will let you know how much weight you can put on your leg — follow that guidance closely even on days your knee feels better than expected, since healing happens on a schedule that isn't always obvious from how you feel.

Gentle motion exercises given to you by your therapist (like heel slides or straightening the knee while lying down) are important even though they can feel uncomfortable. Skipping them can make it harder to regain full motion later. If an exercise causes sharp pain (different from normal soreness or stretch), stop and mention it at your next visit.
